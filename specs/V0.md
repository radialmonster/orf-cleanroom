## S1. Recognising V0 (container)
- S1.1 The TIFF compression tag is 1 on every ORF seen, compressed or not, so it does not tell V0 from anything else. [F-tag]
- S1.2 A payload is V0 exactly when its byte count equals width x height x 12, 12.8 or 16 bits / 8 (`size_class` packed-12, packed-12.8, packed-16). Any other size is "variable" (compressed, V1 and later, not specified here). [F-fixed]
- S1.3 The payload is the set of raw-image strips of the IFD with the largest strip byte total; strips are read in StripOffsets order and concatenated. On 6 of the 9 packed-12 CC0 files there is one gap between strips (1224 to 1624 bytes), so strips must be read individually, not as one contiguous run. The concatenation then equals height x row bytes exactly. [F-layout, partial: measured strip geometry and exact totals on the 17-file cohort; general gap rules remain open, G1]
- S1.4 Row layout on the measured cohort: the 6 packed-12 files with the strip gap support two-field storage, with stored rows 0..n1-1 (n1 = ceil(h/2)) corresponding to even image rows and stored rows n1..h-1 to odd rows; the gap and a stored-row discontinuity occur at n1. Interleaving restores distance-two row continuity over every eligible interior row of the 400 centre columns. The other 11 files support natural order. [F-layout, partial: same files, wider row coverage, not a new body or proof of absolute physical row origin] The decoder chooses layout by comparing stored rows 1 apart with 2 apart; its general selector remains unverified (G1, G6; lead #77 remains open).
- S1.5 On all 17 conformance files the selected raw IFD is IFD0. Orientation is 1 on 16 and 6 on XZ-10 2431. DNGVersion is absent from IFD0, and explicit DNG DefaultScale, DefaultCropOrigin, DefaultCropSize and ActiveArea fields are absent from each selected raw IFD. Other crop declarations and physical raw-to-preview geometry remain open. [F-declared-geometry]
- S1.6 Exif 0xa002/0xa003 are also absent in the visited Exif directories of all 17. The fixed leading-OLYMPUS 0x2040 path gives literal LONG/count-one 0x0614/0x0615 and SHORT/count-four 0x0637 on XZ-2 1432, SH-2 792, PEN-F 2977, XZ-10 2431 and E-M5 Mark II High Res 2856. Their raw-minus-literal dimension differences are respectively 16x10, 16x14, 32x20, 16x14 and 64x20. Twelve other MakerNote scopes remain unknown under this header rule. The tuples are uninterpreted; these differences do not allocate borders or establish a physical crop, coordinate order/origin, scale or preview registration. Orientation and declared geometry repeat S1.5. [F-maker-geometry]

- S1.7 A separate legacy leading-OLYMP directory rule supports top-directory structure on E-1 1800, E-300 2878 and E-500 3540: 27 entries at relative byte 8, end 338, next pointer zero, with 0x2040/0x2050 encoded as UNDEFINED blocks. C5060WZ 1799 has 32 entries, end 398, next zero and neither top entry. C7070WZ 780 remains unsupported under this rule. The block contents and offset bases are unparsed, so these facts establish no inner-tag absence, active dimensions, crop geometry or physical registration. [F-legacy-top; F-legacy-identity]

  On those same three block-bearing files, neither file-absolute nor MakerNote-relative slots give a common pair within the declared envelope. Under the absolute candidate, 0x2050 ends 36 bytes beyond it in all three. The 1799 absence and 780 unsupported controls reproduce. The actual offset base and inner shape remain unknown. [F-legacy-bases]

## S2. packed-12
- S2.1 Two 12-bit values per 3 bytes, most significant bit first: first = b0 << 4 | b1 >> 4, second = (b1 & 15) << 8 | b2. Rows are contiguous with stride width x 1.5 bytes. [F-p12]
- S2.2 Six bodies (C5060WZ, C7070WZ, SP-510UZ, SP-550UZ, SP-565UZ, SP-570UZ) use this order directly. Three (XZ-2, XZ-10, SH-2) first have the bytes reversed inside every 4-byte word. [F-p12]
- S2.3 The reference decoder chooses the order with lower roughness on three bands of up to 16 unpacked rows (S6). The observed header correspondence is in S2.5 and G2. [F-p12]
- S2.4 The order of the two pixels inside a unit is NOT decided by the evidence. The reference decoder emits first, second. Output hashes therefore pin this choice. [F-p12, last sentence of its claim]
- S2.5 Observation (partly a finding, see F-hdr): all 6 plain files have header `IIRS`, all 3 reversed ones `IIRO`. [decoder run; whole-frame pairing header to byte order and strip gap holds on 9 of 9 CC0 files (6 `IIRS` plain, F-hdr; 3 `IIRO` reversed, 1 in F-hdr and XZ-10, SH-2 in F-iiro, roughness reversed 0.025 to 0.058 against plain 0.590 to 0.806 on the two) and 4 of 4 non-CC0 `IIRS` files, F-hdr (partial) and F-iiro (partial); same files as lead #78, so no new body; the rule itself stays a candidate, confounded with camera generation; header versus strip table is not separated; lead #78 open; see gap G2]

- S2.6 Independent reproduction repeats all nine original default/wider family margins, wider true/one-byte-off stride values and the three stated low-two-bit shares, then retains the same families on predeclared 12-band windows. All nine complete reference conformance rows/hashes match, preserving declared strip gaps and known two-field layouts. The original 8*32*2400 window covers 614,400 values, about 10.67 times the 57,600-value default, correcting the original prose's approximate fivefold estimate. The historic window probe is a file-offset measure; full-frame conformance uses the declared-strip reader. Shared tools and the same captures do not resolve physical pair order or general selectors. [F-p12]

## S3. packed-12.8
- S3.1 Rows are runs of 16-byte groups, 10 pixels per group. Bytes 0 to 14 are five 3-byte units; each unit holds two 12-bit values least significant bit first: first = b0 | (b1 & 15) << 8, second = b1 >> 4 | b2 << 4. Byte 15 is padding. Rows are contiguous, stride width x 1.6 bytes. [F-p128] Width is a multiple of 10. [observation on the 5 files]
- S3.2 Byte 15 is 0 in every group of the whole payload on all 5 CC0 files, and each payload is exactly width x height x 1.6 bytes, a whole number of groups. [F-p128] The decoder still reports the count of non-zero pad bytes.
- S3.3 The order of the two pixels inside a unit is not decided (tie within 2%); the decoder emits first, second. [F-p128; gap G4]
- S3.4 Independent reproduction by @mszyndel repeats the default and original 8-band comparisons on all five currently recorded CC0 packed-12.8 inputs, then checks a predeclared 12-band window on the same captures. All select the same layout family; all 17,027,024 whole-payload byte-15 positions are zero with exact group ends. Requested 3200-column runs read 3180 columns. In F-p128-original's Verification prose, the bracketed stride controls are delta -2/-1, not the actual -1/+1 pair; F-p128 gives explicit labels. These reporting corrections do not change packing. Shared tools and reused captures do not resolve physical pair order or provide new-body validation. [F-p128]

## S4. packed-16
- S4.1 One 16-bit word per pixel holding the 12-bit value shifted left by 4; stride width x 2. [F-p16] On the three CC0 files the payload is exactly width x height x 2 bytes, their strips are contiguous, and whole-frame lag-2 roughness jumps at one byte off the true stride (0.064 / 0.641 / 0.642 on E-1, 0.039 / 0.663 / 0.663 on E-400, 0.046 / 0.753 / 0.746 on E-10 at 0 / -1 / +1 byte). [F-p16]
- S4.2 Byte order is per body: E-1 and E-400 little-endian, E-10 big-endian. [F-p16] Whole-frame smoothness (little-endian against big-endian roughness) agrees with the TIFF byte-order mark on 3 of 3: E-1 0.126 against 1.414 and E-400 0.078 against 1.409 (`II`, little-endian smoother), E-10 1.661 against 0.090 (`MM`, big-endian smoother), margins 11.2 to 18.4 [F-p16; the same three files were measured whole-frame in F-iiro, Partial]. E-10 is the only `MMOR` file, so the mark and the file header are not separated. The rule stays a candidate (gap G2). The decoder still autodetects by smoothness.
- S4.3 Output hashes use the stored words, not shifted back. On E-400, 793 words have a non-zero low nibble; E-1 and E-10 have none. 791 of the 792 interior ones equal the floor of the mean of the 4 nearest same-colour neighbours: diagonal-1 at parities (0,0) 218 and (1,1) 187, axial-2 at (0,1) 183 and (1,0) 203, so the stored words are interpolated pixels. [F-e400, partial verification of lead #79; the 793 whole-frame count is confirmed in F-p16; one exception, gap G3]

- S4.4 Independent reproduction matches every published whole-frame count, order score/margin and explicit stride value on the same three public files, and all three complete reference conformance rows/hashes. Nonzero low-nibble counts are 0/793/0 and stored words remain unshifted. This uses the published locator/measure and existing reference decoder; it does not independently establish the physical cause of the exceptions or generalize the byte-order selector beyond the measured cohort. [F-p16]

## S5. Not specified
Everything else (V1, V2, all compressed payloads) is "not yet specified". The reference decoder reports that and decodes nothing.

## S6. Output hash (conformance)
SHA-256 of the decoded values as little-endian uint16, row-major in image row order (S1.4 undone for two-field files), unshifted (no shift applied by the decoder: packed-16 words are hashed as stored, low nibbles included, S4.3), width x height values. Two implementations agree on a file when this hash matches. A second implementation written from this spec alone matches all 17 hashes with exact payload ends [`2026-10-19--radialmonster--independent-v0-hash-agreement` (findings/V0), partial verification of lead #75]. It used the explicit selectors of G6 (header, strip-gap position, TIFF byte-order mark), so on these 17 files those rules and the smoothness choices agree.

A separate contributor's run of the existing reference decoder reproduces all five packed-12.8 conformance rows, including output hashes and exact payload ends. This uses the decoder's emitted pair order and is not a new implementation or proof of physical order. [F-p128]

### Reference selector procedure

The following records the automatic choices in `decoder/orf_decode.py`. The conformance result in S6 and the measured layout and header evidence cover the tested cohort. Generalization, physical row origin and within-pair order remain open. [F-layout, F-hdr, F-iiro, #75, #77, #78]

For order, try `plain` then `word4-reversed` for packed-12, `lsb` alone for packed-12.8, and `le` then `be` for packed-16. Omit `word4-reversed` when row byte count is not divisible by four. If only one choice remains, return it with margin 1. Otherwise use `nr = min(16, height)` stored rows from each of three starts. For k = 0, 1, 2, the start is `min(2 * floor(floor(height * (2*k+1) / 6) / 2), height - nr)`. Starts can repeat. Use the first `floor(min(width, 1200) / 30) * 30` columns, or the full width when that expression is zero.

For each choice, unpack the selected rows. Divide the mean absolute distance-two column difference by the mean of all selected values. A zero mean uses 1 instead. Choose the lowest score, with the earlier choice winning ties. The margin is the next-lowest score divided by the chosen score, or infinity when the chosen score is zero. With multiple choices and a selected width of two, the difference count is zero and scoring raises a division-by-zero error.

For layout, height below 64 returns `natural` with margin 1. Otherwise set `c0 = max(0, floor((width - 400) / 2))` and select columns in the half-open interval `[c0, min(width, c0 + 400))`. For k = 0, 1, 2, use the chosen or requested order to unpack ten stored rows starting at `2 * floor(floor(height * (2*k+1) / 6) / 2) - 4`. In each window, sum absolute differences across the selected columns for eight row pairs at offsets `(i, i+1)` and separately `(i, i+2)`, for i = 0 through 7. Sum each distance across all windows. If the distance-one sum is strictly smaller, return `two-field` with margin distance-two divided by distance-one, replacing a zero denominator with 1. Otherwise return `natural` with margin distance-one divided by distance-two, again replacing a zero denominator with 1. Ties therefore select `natural`, including a zero/zero tie with margin 0.

The `two-field` output maps even image row y to stored row `floor(y/2)` and odd y to `ceil(height/2) + floor(y/2)` (S1.4). The selector uses the declared geometry and stored-row samples.

## S7. Synthetic envelope stress test
The frozen toy development envelope accepts 7/9 identity `floor(stored_word/16)` windows and 16/36 nonidentity controls across the three public CC0 packed-16 files, with two identity rejections. This is a bounded construction-label stress test under the stated scale assumption; it does not establish a correct sensor mapping, physical plausibility, or a decoding rule. The three files and all nine windows are the complete current CC0 packed-16 manifest cohort. [F-toy]

With the original 32x24 limits held fixed, the nested 64x48, 128x96 and 256x192 windows accept respectively 7/9, 6/9 and 5/9 identity controls and 17/36, 13/36 and 12/36 nonidentity controls. The original E-1 middle and E-400 top lag2 rejections persist. E-10 middle also fails at 128x96 and E-10 bottom joins it at 256x192. Size-specific refits give the same reasons through 128x96. At 256x192, refitting accepts 3/9 identities and 6/36 nonidentities. Synthetic values reach 4095 more often in larger training windows, and the refit changes both the training footprint and limits. The overlapping windows come from the same three captures. [F-toy-scale]
